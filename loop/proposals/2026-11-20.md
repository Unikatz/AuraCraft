## Items

### 1. Animals get in the way when placing blocks (validate and act)

- Source: feedback issue 4 (18 Nov, phone, confusing, rated 3 of 5: "My pet still blocks the way",
  no detail). The owner also passed on that animals get in the way. One tester note.
- Problem: build w3 fixed this for Doggy and Mew only. The dev check on 19 Nov (see `BACKLOG.md`)
  shows that a wild creature (such as a Puff-sheep) in the target square still stops the block,
  and pressing Place pets it instead ("likes you! +1 aura"). A wild creature between the player
  and the target also stops the block, often with no message. In the code, Place only skips pets
  when aiming (`skipPets`), and only pets may hop aside (`petHop`). We cannot tell which build the
  tester was on, or whether "pet" meant Doggy/Mew or any animal.
- Proposed change: option B from the backlog. With a block in hand, Place aims past every
  creature (Use still pets them). A wild creature in the target square is not moved: the game
  shows "Something is in the way" so it is never silent. Pets keep the w3 hop. Add tests: a wild
  creature in front of the target, a wild creature in the target square (message, no block),
  and that Use still pets a wild creature.
- Effort: about half a day, tests included.
- Risk: low. No new block, nothing saved changes. A player who liked petting by pressing Place
  with a block in hand will now need Use (that is already how the quick guide teaches it).
- Recommendation: approve option B. It fixes the silent failure whatever the tester meant, and
  moves no wild creature (option A could push a creature into water or lava, like the pet risk
  noted on 17 Nov). Ask in the TL;DR which creature the owner's report was about (V1), and keep
  watching "Tell us" for "My pet got in the way" and "A block won't place".

- What the dev found: the first version (branches `v1-stable`, `v1.1-live`) had zombies. Aura

- Proposed change, split in two:

    About 15 minutes, plus a check in the guide test that Help does not say "zombie".
  - 2b (not for the dev to decide): going back to the first version is a change to the look and
    direction of the game and is far bigger than a week. The dev does not know which parts the
    tester means.
- Effort: 2a about 30 minutes with the test. 2b: unknown, more than one week.
- Risk: 2a very low (words only; the internal name `zombie` in the code and saves stays as it is).
- Recommendation: approve 2a. Send 2b to the owner (direction of the game). A suggested question
  for the owner: should "Tell us" get a preset reason like "I liked the old version better" so we
  learn what testers miss? (That changes the button's preset list, which the owner set.)

### 3. Healthy play: eye and stretch break, plus tips for grown-ups (H1 + H4)

- Source: owner, 16 Nov (backlog, sprint from 20 Nov). Not tester feedback.
- Problem: there is no reminder to take a break; long sessions on a phone are common for this age.
- Proposed change:
  - H1: after 30 minutes of play without a break, the game saves and shows a calm screen (your pet
    wants a stretch): look at something far away for 20 seconds, stand up, have some water. A
    20-second countdown, then Keep playing. The timer resets after the break or after 5 minutes
    away (the game already saves when the tab is hidden). The timer lives only in memory for the
    session; nothing new is saved.
  - H4: a short "For grown-ups" section in Help: a bigger screen at arm's length, a break every
    30 minutes, no screens at meals or in the hour before bed, time outdoors every day. The
    wording is checked against the MOH Grow Well SG and AAP pages before it ships, as the backlog
    asks, and kept to plain advice (no medical claims).
- Effort: H1 about 1 day with a test (fake clock: break at 30 min, reset after 5 min away, Keep
  playing works); H4 about 2 hours. About 1.25 days in all.
- Risk: low. Nothing leaves the device, nothing saved changes. A pause in the middle of a fight
  with Gloomies could feel unfair: the break waits until no Gloomy is within 10 blocks (the same
  rule the bed uses), up to 2 extra minutes.
- Recommendation: approve.

### 4. Healthy play: evening wind-down note (H2)

- Source: owner, 16 Nov (backlog). Not tester feedback.
- Problem: nothing in the game nudges toward bedtime.
- Proposed change: after 8 pm on the device's own clock, one gentle note per evening: "It's getting
  late. Time to tuck Doggy and Mew into bed soon." It does not block play. "Once per evening" is
  kept in memory for the session plus one small device-only flag with today's date, stored under
  its own key, not inside the world save.
- Effort: about half a day with a test (fake clock at 19:59 and 20:01; shown once).
- Risk: low. The 8 pm time is the dev's first pick; no parent setting in this slice. The flag is a
  date on the device only (see item 5 for the reading on that).
- Recommendation: approve.

### 5. Healthy play: daily play total with a soft wrap-up (H3)

- Source: owner, 16 Nov (backlog). Not tester feedback.
- Problem: nothing tells a child they have played a long time today.
- Proposed change: the game counts today's play minutes on the device only, under its own storage
  key (not inside the world save, so the save format and the rollback check are untouched). At
  60 minutes it shows "Great building today! Time for a real-world adventure", once per day, with
  Keep playing. The count resets with the date. Nothing is shown to anyone else or sent anywhere.
- Effort: about half a day with a test.
- Risk: low in code. Open point the backlog asked to raise: this keeps a record about the
  player's play time on the device. The charter's current reading (device-only is not data
  collection) allows it, but the owner has not confirmed that reading. The dev suggests the
  Approver send this to the owner to confirm, even though the owner asked for the item.
- Recommendation: escalate for the owner to confirm the reading; build it the same week if the
  owner says yes before Tuesday.

### 6. Unknown blocks never stop a world from opening (R2)

- Source: owner request (rollback safety, backlog). Not tester feedback.
- Problem: the rollback test (16 Nov) showed that a world holding a block this version does not
  know fails to open. The "new blocks ship unplaceable first" rule prevents this today; R2 is a
  second line of defence.
- Proposed change: when a save holds a block id this version does not know, draw it as a plain
  placeholder block and keep the id in the save unchanged, so the world opens and nothing is lost
  if the player later moves to a newer build. Add the case to `tests/rollback.js`.
- Effort: about half a day with the test.
- Risk: medium-low. It does not change what is saved, but it changes how the game reads a save,
  which is close to the charter's "save format" line. The dev reads it as a load-safety fix, not a
  format change, and flags it for the Approver to judge. Note it only protects builds from this one
  onward: `stable` today still cannot open such worlds, so the "unplaceable first" rule stays.
- Recommendation: approve, with the Approver checking it against the save-format line.
